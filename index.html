Mac Freeware

Small, free and open-source tools for macOS

Every app here started because something on my own Mac was missing, slow or awkward. Once a tool made my daily work easier, I shared it: free, with its source code, under the GNU GPL.

Apps

MacMonitor

Menu bar CPU, RAM, network, disk and battery monitor

MacMonitor is a free, open-source macOS menu bar app that shows live CPU, RAM and network speed, with per-core load, memory breakdown, top processes, network usage history, free disk space and a long battery history one click away.

A lightweight, free alternative to iStat Menus, Stats and MenuMeters when you only need CPU, memory, disk and battery at a glance.

MacCalendar

Menu bar calendar and world clock

MacCalendar is a free, open-source macOS menu bar calendar with a dual world clock and country flags, a month view, quick notes for any day and more than 400 cities.

A free alternative to Itsycal, Dato and Clocker: a month calendar and a second time zone right in the menu bar.

Download (pkg) Details

MacRAR

WinRAR-style archive manager

MacRAR is a free, open-source, WinRAR-style archive manager for macOS: open, extract and create RAR, ZIP, 7Z and TAR archives, with a Finder right-click menu.

A free WinRAR alternative for the Mac, and an alternative to Keka, BetterZip and The Unarchiver.

Download (pkg) Details

MacZilla

FTP, FTPS and SFTP client

MacZilla is a free, open-source, native FTP, FTPS and SFTP client for macOS in the spirit of FileZilla: two panes, tabs, a transfer queue, folder sync and a Site Manager.

A free, native alternative to FileZilla, Cyberduck and Transmit on the Mac.

Download (zip) Details

MacExplorer

Windows Explorer-style file manager

MacExplorer is a free, open-source, Windows Explorer-style file manager for macOS: tabs, dual pane, real Cut and Paste, a preview pane, and SFTP and FTP right in the window.

A free alternative to Finder, Path Finder, ForkLift and Commander One for people who miss Windows Explorer on the Mac.

What they have in common

  • Free and open source under the GNU GPL, version 3 or later. No ads, no accounts, no in-app purchases.
  • Native Mac apps written in Swift and SwiftUI; no Electron. They run on Apple Silicon and Intel Macs.
  • In English and Turkish.

Before you install

The apps are not notarized by Apple (that needs a paid developer account), so macOS blocks them the first time. Allow each app once in System Settings ▸ Privacy & Security ▸ Open Anyway. Every release lists a SHA-256 checksum.
